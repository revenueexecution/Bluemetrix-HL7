The problem

Standard PHI discovery checks the fields it already knows about. HL7 v2 doesn't stay inside them.

HL7 v2's flexibility — the same trait that let it survive thirty years of hospital IT — is exactly why identifiers end up in places no schema anticipated.

Structured, but inconsistent

PID and PV1 segments carry the obvious identifiers, but optional-field usage varies hospital to hospital and vendor to vendor.

Free text, unbounded

OBX and NTE narrative fields are where clinicians actually write — names, relatives and phone numbers with no structure to catch them.

Custom Z-segments

Hospital-specific extensions carry local identifiers — SSNs, referring physicians, case numbers — with no standard mapping.

Embedded documents

Base64 attachments — discharge summaries, referral letters, imaging reports — travel as opaque blobs field-level discovery never opens.

⚠ DSPM and DLP tooling built for structured databases treats these HL7 feeds as a blind spot — remediation happens at rest, after the fact, if it happens at all.

Tokenising every non-production environment by default removes that risk class rather than relying on process discipline to prevent it.
